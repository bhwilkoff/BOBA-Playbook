Play

Goal

Best-of-7 Battles. Higher Power wins each Battle. First player to win 4 Battles wins the game. A game can end as early as Battle 4 (4–0 sweep).

Setup

  • Each player shuffles their 60-card Hero Deck and places it face-down.
  • Each player places 7 Heroes face-down in a row — these are the 7 battle slots.
  • Flip a coin to determine first Honors — the right to act first.
  • Heroes are inspired by real athletes — always say "inspired by," never "is" or "based on."

Each Battle

  • Both players simultaneously flip their Hero face-up.
  • The player with the higher Power wins the battle.
  • Tie → Sudden Death: each player reveals the top card of their Hero Deck; higher Power wins. Repeat until resolved.
Substitution adds: Hot Dog Deck (10 cards)

Additional Setup

  • After placing your 7 face-down Heroes, draw the rest of your Hero Deck into your hand.
  • Draw 10 Hot Dogs into your Hot Dog pile.

Substitution Window

  • After both Heroes are revealed, the Honors player decides first whether to substitute.
  • Cost: 2 Hot Dogs — send the revealed Hero to the Discard Pile and play a Hero from hand face-up.
  • The other player then decides. Each player may substitute at most once per battle.

Honors

Moves to the winner of each battle. Having Honors means you act first — and you see your opponent's substitution before deciding your own. Sometimes it's better to pass and save Hot Dogs.

Resource Note

You only have 10 Hot Dogs for the entire game. Save substitutions for battles where the power gap is meaningful.

Playmaker adds: Playbook (30 unique Play cards)

Additional Setup

  • Shuffle your Playbook. Draw 5 Plays as your starting hand.
  • Draw 1 Play at the start of each subsequent battle.

Play Window

  • After the Substitution Window, the Honors player may play Play cards.
  • Pay the Hot Dog cost shown on the card and resolve its effect.
  • Players alternate playing Plays until both pass.

Super Weapon Tiebreaker

In Playmaker mode only — if a battle ends in a tie and one Hero has the Super weapon type, that Hero wins automatically (no Sudden Death needed).

Tournament Note

Playmaker is the competitive and tournament-standard format.

SPEC Format

A special tournament variant where no Hero in the deck may have a base Power above 160. Players may build a sideboard of up to 45 Plays to swap between games in a match.

Power Curve Management

The 6-per-power-value rule forces you to spread across multiple power levels. Build with intention:

  • High-power Heroes (160–200) — for critical battles you cannot afford to lose
  • Mid-range (120–155) — consistency and volume
  • Lower-power Heroes (85–115) — inevitable, but position them wisely

Place battle slots strategically — front-load to establish Honors momentum, or back-load to close games. Battle 7 is do-or-die; consider saving a strong Hero. Plays like Late Hit (+35 in Battle 7) and The Closer (+40 in Battle 7) reward this approach.

Weapon Synergy

Many Plays reward weapon-type consistency. Build around 1–2 primary weapon types:

  • Fire: Fire Boost, Fire Crew, Flame Wall, Burning Fever, Eternal Flame, Smitty
  • Ice: Ice Boost, Ice Crew, Icy Shield, Frozen Resolve, Frozen Lineup, Unbreakable Ice
  • Steel: Steel Boost, Steel Crew, Steel Defense, Steel Shield, Chrome Will, Steel Cage
  • Mixed: Use weapon-agnostic and opponent-targeting Plays — maximum flexibility, but no synergy stack as powerful as a focused build
Substitution Strategy

You only have 10 Hot Dogs for the entire game — shared between substitutions (2 each) and Play cards. Don't substitute reflexively.

  • Substitute when the power gap is large enough to justify the cost
  • Watch your opponent's Hot Dog count — at 0, they can neither substitute nor play cards
  • Save substitutions for battles 5–7, which are often the most decisive
  • Having Honors means you substitute first and reveal information; sometimes it's strategically better to pass
Play Card Types
  • Tempo: Immediate power boosts. Example: Buff Up 15 (+15 for 2 Hot Dogs). Straightforward but costly.
  • Value: Ongoing effects. Example: Fire Boost (+10 to all Fire Heroes rest of game). Higher long-term return.
  • Disruption: Deny opponent options. Example: Full Court Press (opponent can't play this battle), Bench Blocker (-20 power + no substitution).
  • Economy: Recover Hot Dogs. Example: Trash Bandit (recover Hot Dogs free), Victory Dinner (recover 3 on a win). Sustain your resource engine.
  • Game-changers: Edge Rush (5 cost — set your power to 5 above opponent's current), Deadline Deal (3 cost — swap powers with opponent), By Any Means Necessary (6 cost — search and play any Play card for free).
Resource Management

You start with 5 Plays in hand and draw 1 per battle (7 total draws across a full 7-battle game).

  • Hot Dogs fuel both substitutions and Plays — don't exhaust them on early battles
  • Free (0-cost) Plays are disproportionately valuable
  • Track the opponent's Hot Dog count openly; knowing they're depleted changes every decision
